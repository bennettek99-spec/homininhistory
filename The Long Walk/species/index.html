The human family tree

The hominin species

Meet the ancestors and cousins on the human family tree — from Sahelanthropus, seven million years ago, to Homo sapiens today. Each species has its own full report: anatomy, brain, behaviour and the key fossils.

~7 million years agoSahelanthropus tchadensisToumaïAt the very root of the family tree, an ape that may have already stood up.
~4.4 million years agoArdipithecus ramidusArdiA walker in the woods, still keeping one grasping foot in the trees.
3.9 – 2.9 million years agoAustralopithecus afarensisLucy's speciesConfidently bipedal on the ground — and the maker of footprints frozen in ash.
3.3 – 2.1 million years agoAustralopithecus africanusTaung Child's speciesThe fossil that first convinced the world humanity began in Africa.
2.4 – 1.5 million years agoHomo habilisHandy ManThe first of our own genus — and, perhaps, the first deliberate toolmaker named for it.
2.3 – 1.2 million years agoParanthropus boiseiNutcracker ManA specialist of the chewing apparatus — and a dead end on the tree.
1.9 million – ~110,000 years agoHomo erectusUpright ManThe great pioneer: the first human to walk out of Africa, master fire, and endure for over a million years.
~700,000 – 200,000 years agoHomo heidelbergensisHeidelberg ManThe crossroads species — big-brained hunters whose descendants would split into Neanderthals, Denisovans and us.
~430,000 – 40,000 years agoHomo neanderthalensisNeanderthalsCold-adapted, big-brained and deeply human — our closest kin, and partly inside our own DNA.
335,000 – 236,000 years agoHomo nalediStar ManA tiny-brained human that survived into our own age — and may have buried its dead in the dark.
~300,000 years ago – presentHomo sapiensModern Humans — usThe last human standing — a symbol-making, story-telling, world-spanning ape.
~200,000 – 30,000 years agoHomo sp. (Denisovan)DenisovansA whole human species first discovered not from a skull, but from a fingertip's DNA.
~100,000 – 50,000 years agoHomo floresiensisThe HobbitA one-metre-tall human that hunted dwarf elephants on a lost island world.
